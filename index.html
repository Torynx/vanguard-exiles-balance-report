Vanguard Exiles · Community Balance Report · August 2026

Unlimited Unit Balance Report & Rebalance Proposal

How balanced are the free basic units against each other? This August 2026 release measures every known stat configuration the same way — simulated fights for every unit pair, from 1v1 to 6v6 — and offers a community rebalance: one recommended composition with per-unit implementation choices. Every number in this page comes from those simulations.

Download the full workbook (.xlsx) for every win rate, sample size, and stat sheet.

The one-line version: today, 72% of basic-unit matchups are effectively decided the moment both sides deploy. The proposal cuts that to 1% — without removing a single mechanic.

Executive Summary

The current patch, Main v0.3.11.4021, remains the most one-sided state measured: three out of four basic-unit matchups are 90/10 or worse. The recommended rebalance is the Round 2 proposal in the table above — one composition with selectable implementation choices per unit. Nothing sits above 7 health, no ability gains a hard limit, every death still feeds both group-fight engines, and every 1v1 between the melee basics becomes a near coin flip. The first-round Options A-D remain in the ranking for comparison.

Design Notes & Rationale

The complete change list, choices, and formulas are in the table at the top. This section explains why each change is shaped the way it is.

The statlines are chosen so the melee basics trade in equal hit counts — including through Nail's armor. The 1v1 grid stays a field of coin flips; the only intended spread is Elf Scout's evade edges. Group fights then differ by each unit's mechanic — and the two Red basics land at the same power as different tools: Zombie clusters and converts kills into bodies, Skeleton spreads and makes its removal cost tempo.

How Lodged plays: killing a Skeleton in melee stuns the killer — it loses its next attack — three times out of four. It is deterministic enough to plan around — remove skeletons with your least tempo-critical unit, or shoot them (ranged kills avoid the grip entirely, so ranged removal becomes the clean answer to bone walls). It never changes a 1v1 outcome, because the fight is already decided when it procs. Area and cleave attackers were measured to pay the same per-kill tax as single-target attackers — multi-kills in one action trigger the grip once, never stacked.

How the gated plague plays: a real Zombie's kill starts the horde 50% of the time; from there every raised body keeps the live 100% chain, so the horde grows at exactly live speed once it exists. The gate never decides a duel — the proc fires on the killing blow, when the fight is already won — what it prices is the free body the winner always used to collect on top of winning. Gating only the first rise is what lets every raised body stay completely untouched: without a plague ability at all, Zombie measures a fair duelist that collapses in group fights, and at a 100% first rise it tops the pool — the gate is the single dial between those poles.

HammerStatline unchanged from live; the pool racing it evenly removes its dominance, and the on-kill swing (an existing mechanic at half rate — swings chain, as in the live game) gives it an identity and lifts it to mid-pack.
NailIdentity untouched, one more health point. Deliberately the weakest basic in big brawls; its role is armor utility — two Nails beat a Skeleton Knight outright, and Squire's ambush opener remains a clean answer to Nail walls.
SkeletonGets its original 3 attack back; ranged evade softens from a hard lock to a lean. Lodged makes felling it in melee cost tempo: it never touches 1v1 outcomes, taxes focus-fire in groups, and ranged removal avoids the grip — the two halves cover both lanes without either being a hard counter.
ZombieLast Strike made its 1v1s all-or-nothing; attacking in the normal beat order plus 7 HP makes them real fights. The plague's first rise is gated at 50%; the raised horde itself is untouched and chains at live speed. Nothing else that raises zombies changes.
AcolyteEvery 100% design that keeps the attack-Reap unlimited was measured and saturates big fights regardless of the statline carrying it; keeping only the healing half is the one deterministic shape that balances. Every death still pays — the Acolyte grows fat on souls instead of sharp — it holds the pool's lowest one-sided rate, and the change is a single ability-list cell, so the Magi and Possessed Acolyte keep the full harvest untouched. A measured Magus check confirmed durable Acolytes feed an allied High Magus no more than the other designs do.
Elf ScoutKeeps 5 HP (the 4-HP beta version crosses two one-shot/two-shot breakpoints at once); evade is the fine-grained survivability dial.
RangerAttack 3 restores its punch (the pre-rework value); 20% evade keeps the test build's survivability buff.

What was tried and rejected: Ambush on Hammer (its 4-damage opener forced three other units up to 8 health, costing about six balance points). Taunt and targeting-override mechanics (too much influence on targeting). Reactive on-hit abilities — counterattacks and stun-when-struck — which turned out to have no viable setting: at rates high enough to notice they break even 1v1s and punish multi-hit attackers hardest, and at safe rates they fire too rarely to matter. Moving the trigger to the unit's death (Lodged) is what escaped that trap. An 8th health point on Hammer or Nail was also measured and found to buy almost nothing once the group-fight engines are proc-tuned. For Acolyte, the whole 100%-reliability space was measured before settling on the healing-half design: guaranteed one-resource-per-death variants (attack-only, attack-or-HP splits at either exchange rate) all land the Acolyte at .69-.75 with big fights at .90+; a low-attack statline with an Ambush opener recreates the live helpless-small/unbeatable-big curve in both directions; a self-consuming +1-attack/−1-HP harvest annihilates its own carrier (each victory can kill the winner on the enemy's dying breath); and a 3-stack cap re-ignites the big-fight inferno where the 2-stack cap does not. For Zombie, three whole families were measured and rejected before settling on gating the first rise: uniform proc-gating with a weakened new "Lesser Zombie" unit (balanced the row, but added a unit and slowed the horde everywhere); statline deficits (a 3/6 Zombie starves the very engine it has to ignite, and a 4/6 bruiser balances on average only by being helpless early and dominant late); and weaker raised bodies (below 2 attack the chain almost never advances — a decorative horde).

Methodology

Show how the numbers were made

The balance framework measures more than average win rate: it looks for broadly playable matchups, few extreme stomps, small strongest-to-weakest gaps, and low power dispersion. Metric definitions are unchanged from the previous release and are included in the workbook.

Every configuration was tested with the same matchups: repeated-unit one-zone fights for every unit pair and team size, up to 10,000 simulated fights per matchup. Sample sizes for every cell are in the workbook.
