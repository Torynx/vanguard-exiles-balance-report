Vanguard Exiles · Community Balance Report · August 2026

Unlimited Unit Balance Report & Rebalance Proposal

How balanced are the free basic units against each other? This August 2026 release measures every known stat configuration the same way — simulated fights for every unit pair, from 1v1 to 6v6 — and offers a community rebalance: a recommended proposal plus two lighter options. Every number in this page comes from those simulations.

Download the full workbook (.xlsx) for every win rate, sample size, and stat sheet.

The one-line version: today, 72% of basic-unit matchups are effectively decided the moment both sides deploy. The proposal cuts that to 5% — without removing a single mechanic.

Executive Summary

The current patch, Main v0.3.11.4021, remains the most one-sided state measured: three out of four basic-unit matchups are 90/10 or worse. The recommended rebalance is Option B below — nodnarb21's preferred configuration, with the fewest one-sided matchups measured — alongside three variants sharing the same stat changes: down to stats-only, and Option A, the single most balanced state, for developers who prefer no new Hammer mechanic. Every option keeps every unit's signature mechanic and turns almost every 1v1 between the melee basics into a near coin flip; the deeper options also tame the group-fight snowballs.

The Proposed Options (August 2026)

Every unit keeps its signature mechanic. The recommended Option B (nodnarb21's favorite) changes a few attack and health values, two evade percentages, attack timing for two units, a limit on Reap stacking, and the raised zombie's stats — plus one addition: Hammer gains a 50% chance of an immediate extra swing whenever it kills (an existing mechanic at half strength).

Hammer
3/7 → 3/8, Berserk 50% on-kill swing

One more health point, plus a 50% chance to swing again whenever it kills — it finally has a mechanic of its own.

Nail
2/6, 1 armor, Piercing → 2/8, 1 armor, Piercing

Two more health points; its fights become even trades, and armor and Piercing are untouched.

Skeleton
2/7, 50% ranged evade → 3/7, 35% ranged evade

Gets its attack back; ranged evade eases to 35% (the exact number is flexible).

Zombie
3/6, Last Strike, Zombie Plague → 3/7, Zombie Plague

No longer attacks last; raised zombies come back weaker.

Elf Scout
3/5, First Strike, 10% evade → 3/5, First Strike, 25% evade

Keeps 5 health; dodges a lot more.

Acolyte
2/6, Last Strike → 3/7

Fights properly alone; Reap feeds on the first two deaths, not forever.

Ranger
2/4, 10% evade → 3/4, 20% evade

Hits harder, with the test build's 20% evade.

The statlines are chosen so the melee basics trade in equal hit counts — including through Nail's armor. Twelve of the fifteen melee 1v1s land within one point of 50/50; the only spread is Elf Scout's intended evade edges (it wins 56-58% of three matchups and 43% against Acolyte). Group fights then differ by each unit's mechanic.

The other options: A, C, and D

Option A — the most balanced state: identical to Option B with the Hammer swing removed. It posts the single highest Balance Score (72.8 vs 71.7) but more one-sided matchups (9.3% stomps vs 5.3%), and Hammer returns to the bottom half of the pool with no mechanic of its own. nodnarb21's preference is Option B; Option A is the pick if no new Hammer behavior is wanted.

The two lighter options are adoptable fallbacks, not just diagnostics. Option C keeps the stat changes and the weaker raised zombies but drops both the Reap charge limit and the Hammer swing (Reap keeps unlimited stacking). Option D also leaves raised zombies at full strength, making it a stats-and-timing-only change with no ability edits beyond the evade-ranged percentage. Both remain far ahead of every live and historical state; the cost is that Acolyte's 6v6 win rate climbs back toward ~99% (melee-only Balance Score 66.8 for Option C and 66.3 for Option D, vs 71.7 for Option B). If the developers prefer fewer mechanic changes, either is a defensible first step, with the Reap limit and the swing available later as follow-up dials.

What was tried and rejected for Hammer: Ambush (the Knight/Squire opener) was tested first. Its 4-damage opening swing two-shots any 7-health unit, so making it fair forced three other units up to 8 health — and that extra health fed Acolyte's and Zombie's big-fight strength, costing about six balance points overall. The on-kill swing gives Hammer the same "finally has a mechanic" payoff without touching anyone else's stats. Taunt and targeting-override mechanics were also considered and dropped for changing the game's targeting behavior too broadly.

On Ranger: the 3 attack / 4 health / 20% evade line is simply the measured suggestion — the author has no preference among reasonable Ranger buffs, and any of the tested variants works alongside this set.

Methodology

Show how the numbers were made

The balance framework measures more than average win rate: it looks for broadly playable matchups, few extreme stomps, small strongest-to-weakest gaps, and low power dispersion. Metric definitions are unchanged from the previous release and are included in the workbook.

Every configuration was tested with the same matchups: repeated-unit one-zone fights for every unit pair and team size, up to 10,000 simulated fights per matchup. Sample sizes for every cell are in the workbook.
